# Core article review

## Reader task and value

Identify the intended reader, useful outcome and main purpose. Check whether the article actually enables that outcome, with clear limitations and enough detail. Useful synthesis, comparison, explanation or an effective tool can add value; do not require original research, a novelty claim, minimum length or exclusive experience on every page.

Evaluate observable accuracy, originality, usefulness and execution. Do not infer actual effort, honesty or expertise from polished writing, sentence variety, an AI detector or time spent. E-E-A-T is a contextual rater concept, not a measurable Google score or a named single ranking factor. Missing public reputation is not proof of poor reputation.

## Claim verification

Keep the following distinct (a compact working list is enough; don't force four tables):
- Google-source evidence: what the official policy/guidance says.
- Article-claim evidence: what supports this particular fact, comparison or recommendation.
- Page observations: artifact version, exact URL/line/paragraph/selector/JSON pointer, tool and observation time.
- Owner/process evidence: who supplied authorship, testing, permission and review information; what is independently verified versus only stated.

Prioritize claims that carry the conclusion or could cause harm. Use appropriate primary evidence, current prices/features/laws/medical guidance when relevant, and specify period, geography, denominator, units, comparison baseline and conditions. A Google guideline does not substantiate a health effect, product specification or business result. Source access alone does not establish support: read the relevant section and compare its actual scope to the article sentence.

Mark unsupported claims UNKNOWN. Mark FAIL when concrete evidence contradicts them or the artifact demonstrably misrepresents available evidence. A missing test log means testing is unverified, not proven fabricated. Correlation, projections, estimates and recommendations need their proper framing, not false factual certainty.

For calculations, recompute from supplied inputs. Missing inputs are UNKNOWN; an arithmetic error with known inputs can be FAIL. Do not cite a study for a different population, a relative percentage as absolute probability, or a product's best-case result as a universal guarantee.

## Authorship, risk and transparency

Verify any claimed creator identity, credentials, firsthand use, review process, organization and incentives. Ask for real evidence or narrow the wording instead of adding imaginary experts, biographies or test photos. Explain when a reader would reasonably need to know how an AI-assisted or automated result was produced; do not impose universal AI labels.

Scale evidence/review requirements to potential harm and specificity. Consequential medical treatment, legal or financial recommendations may need appropriately qualified review. Merely mentioning a health or money topic does not make professional sign-off mandatory for every sentence. Clearly label any chosen sign-off requirement as an internal risk standard unless an applicable official product policy actually requires it.

Do not assume the user owns or can publish supplied text/images. Flag a concrete rights issue or unknown permission when material; never certify copyright/legal compliance from Google eligibility documents. Do not expose private material while fact-checking it.

## Faithful optimization

Respect a requested structure or stylistic voice and retain effective passages. Suggested changes should remove a concrete problem or improve a reader's ability to complete the task. Prefer:
1. Correct a fact from verified evidence.
2. Limit its scope to what the evidence supports.
3. Remove an unsupported claim if the argument survives without it.
4. Ask one specific evidence question if it is essential.

Do not transform an admitted non-test into a firsthand review. A factual source is not permission to impersonate its author. If the user wants no external links in the public article, keep the audit's source list separate; explain any consequential attribution problem rather than secretly adding links or erasing its evidence trail. Do not invent or assume such a link preference for other users.

If the user asks for a finished article and important claims remain unresolved, distinguish the safe revised text from withheld/provisional passages. Do not bury blockers below a polished draft or retain an unsupported claim merely by appending a generic disclaimer.

Review metadata as article claims: a false title, author credential, meta-description promise, image caption, alt text or rating markup remains a content problem even without rendered HTML. Do not clear the article by fixing only its body.
