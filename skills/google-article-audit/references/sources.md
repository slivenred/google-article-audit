# Official sources, versions and limitations

## Authority and scope

The registry contains successfully read official sources cached at creation (2026-10-08), selected for article review and conditional publishing surfaces. It is neither every Google product policy nor a substitute for live verification of changing requirements. The operational checks are derived audit methods, not Google's own rubric.

Keep rule identities separate:
- search_policy: current applicable Search spam/content policy; don't declare a Google penalty without account evidence.
- technical_eligibility: crawl/index/access requirements, not guaranteed inclusion.
- surface_requirement: a particular feature or product's eligibility/policy, with its actual geographic/language scope.
- official_recommendation: recommended practice; do not restate “consider” as mandatory.
- rater_concept: Page Quality, purpose, Needs Met, E-E-A-T; not a direct ranking formula.
- internal_standard: evidence gates, editorial risk labels, professional review choices, version control or permission checks.

A finding can cite multiple kinds of source, but identify the authority actually used. A local requirement for an evidence log is not a Google requirement just because it supports helpful-content recommendations.

## Retrieval and dates

Use the selector to retrieve relevant IDs/URLs/notes, then open the current official page for decisions that depend on current policy or eligibility. Check only applicable documents; don't rerun the entire research catalog each time. Search official sites for a relevant missing source rather than using an SEO blog as authority. Article-fact research uses suitable primary sources outside Google too.

Record title, URL, relevant heading/page, successful read date and language. Distinguish publication date, announced change date, effective date, page footer update and your access date. A new footer doesn't prove when a sentence was added. Do not manufacture date fields absent from a source.

For unreadable, 403/404, empty or unavailable pages: keep the prior successful read date, state cached/unverified-current status, and mark affected uncertainty. Do not report deletion, retirement or a current successful check from a failed request. Use another official format/version if available, without evading access restrictions. A Google update-log entry can establish retirement or documented change when a removed page cannot.

## Conflicts worth checking

- Current Search spam policy, including site-reputation wording and region-specific application, governs policy judgments. Older QRG examples and historical launch posts don't override it. QRG can still explain rater concepts with its own version.
- Newer specific AI optimization, inclusion-control and AI-performance pages may update older ai-features overview statements. Match the product and feature; don't repeat an older “no separate AI report” claim after verifying a newer official report.
- Google News article help and Article structured data have different scope. A News-specific headline suggestion does not impose a universal Search title maximum.
- The current Search Gallery/update log determines supported rich-result types. Historic FAQ/HowTo instructions are not ongoing eligibility evidence. Recipe instruction types are a separate concern.
- Official sources can disagree about specific tools' redirect behavior. Inspect actual evidence and label the unresolved tool-specific conflict; don't conceal it with an invented universal rule.

A later date alone doesn't override higher-authority policy or a different product. Prefer the specific, applicable current source when an explicit change resolves the conflict. Otherwise mark only the affected conclusion UNKNOWN and continue unaffected review.

## Maintenance boundaries

During ordinary use, record newly verified facts in the task's audit output; do not silently edit/install this skill, its source cache or other settings. Updating the skill is a separate user-authorized operation. Cached guidance never authorizes external publication, account changes or sending sensitive article data to a third party.
